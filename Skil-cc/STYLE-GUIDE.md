# SKIL-CC Full 3D Visual Style Guide

## Visual target
Create original stylized cinematic 3D animation with a clear visual hierarchy, expressive characters, readable action and consistent production design. Aim for the level of polish and clarity of professional 3D explainer storytelling, without copying another channel's characters, shots, scripts or signature designs.

## Full-3D rule
Characters, hair, clothes, shoes, props, architecture, terrain, vegetation and effects must be volumetric 3D assets. No 2D character cards, sprites, flat cutouts or billboard people. Background matte paintings may be used only as distant environmental extensions when they do not replace required 3D sets or props.

## Character design
- Distinct silhouette, head/body proportions and recognizable face.
- Hair modeled as volumes or groomed geometry, not a flat texture card.
- Clothing modeled or fitted as geometry with believable folds and thickness.
- Simple rounded mitten hands without separate fingers by default.
- Grounded feet and consistent body scale.
- Consistent face, hair, costume, accessories and materials across all shots.
- Facial expressions must read at the intended camera distance.

## Environment and props
Build real geometry with believable scale, depth, contact points and navigable blocking. Use foreground, midground and background layers made from actual 3D assets. Keep object origins and stable IDs. Story-critical props must remain consistent across shots.

## Materials
Use PBR materials with controlled roughness, restrained specular and coherent colour scripts. Separate skin, cloth, hair, metal, glass, wood, concrete and organic materials. Avoid accidental plastic skin, noisy textures and excessive saturation.

## Cinematography
Choose shot size and lens for dramatic purpose. Establish geography before close coverage. Maintain screen direction, eyelines and camera axis. Use camera movement only when it reveals information, follows action or supports emotion. Keep focus and depth of field intentional.

## Animation
Use anticipation, weight shift, arcs, contact, follow-through, overlap and recovery. Keep feet planted when supporting weight and hands attached to objects during contact. Avoid sliding, floating, clipping, intersections and robotic timing. Facial acting and eye target must support the line being spoken.

## Lighting and effects
Use motivated key/fill/rim and practical sources. Preserve light direction across continuous shots. Effects need a visible cause and interaction with the environment. Keep the action and dialogue readable.

## Sound
Prioritize clean dialogue. Add ambience, Foley, effects, music and silence intentionally. Do not let music mask speech. Sync mouth animation to final approved audio.
