# Character Standard — Low-Poly 3D, Medium Detail

## Non-negotiable result

Every final character is a complete, volumetric 3D model made and saved in Blender. A 2D illustration, billboard, stickman, flat cut-out, or unrigged primitive mannequin is not an acceptable final character.

## Shape language

- Low-poly geometry with controlled, intentional silhouettes.
- Medium detail: enough geometry and surface definition to read the face, hair, clothing layers, footwear, and accessories clearly at the delivery resolution.
- Use clean bevels and weighted normals where useful; avoid noisy microgeometry.
- Maintain appealing, coherent anatomy and recognizable proportions.
- Do not equate low-poly with crude cubes or random disconnected primitives.

## Required character parts

- Head with readable cranium, jaw, ears, neck, and face planes.
- Eyes, eyelids or eye shells, brows, nose, and mouth.
- Hair built from coherent low-poly masses/clumps, not a single featureless cap.
- Torso, pelvis, upper/lower arms, hands, upper/lower legs, and feet.
- Clothing with distinct forms/layers where visible in the reference.
- Shoes and story-required accessories.

Hands should be modeled to the complexity required by the supplied action. Do not force individual fingers when the art direction/reference uses simplified mitten-like hands.

## Rigging

- Stable armature with root, pelvis, spine, chest, neck, head, shoulders, upper/lower arms, hands, upper/lower legs, feet.
- Use IK/FK controls when practical for walking, reaching, and prop interaction.
- Apply transforms and sensible origins before rigging.
- Test deformation at shoulders, elbows, hips, knees, and neck.
- Include foot contact and hand/prop attachment controls where required.
- Facial controls must expose jaw, blink, brows, and speech mouth shapes.

## Identity and reference matching

For each character, derive a written design sheet from the supplied image/script:
- visible face shape, hair silhouette/colour, skin tone, body proportions, outfit, shoes, palette, and unique accessories.
- Separate observed facts from details that are not visible.
- Do not invent a major identity feature that contradicts the reference.
- Keep the same model, materials, proportions, and outfit across all shots unless the script specifies a change.
- Create front, side, and three-quarter turntables plus expression tests.

## Final asset checks

- Model reads correctly from front, side, and three-quarter views.
- Silhouette is distinct from other cast members.
- No visible mesh gaps, floating pieces, severe intersections, or broken normals.
- Rig moves without severe collapse.
- Face and mouth shapes remain readable at final camera distance.
- The model file can be reopened in Blender and all linked assets resolve.
