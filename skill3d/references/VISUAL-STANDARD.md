# Visual Standard — Skill3D

## Default style
**Low-poly 3D, medium detail.** This is the default look for characters, props, and environments unless the user supplies another approved style reference.

## Meaning
Low-poly is a geometry and shading direction, not a story genre:
- Controlled polygon count and clean readable silhouettes.
- Medium-level detail on faces, clothing, props, architecture, and surfaces.
- Carefully placed bevels, normals, material variation, and lighting.
- Avoid both crude primitive-only placeholders and excessive photorealistic microdetail.

## World and genre
The world follows the user's script. Modern post-apocalyptic settings should look like a damaged version of the modern real world:
- Contemporary homes, apartments, shops, offices, schools, hospitals, roads, bridges, cars, buses, motorcycles, utilities, and public infrastructure.
- Damage, debris, broken glass, abandoned vehicles, failed streetlights, overgrowth, weathering, dust, smoke, and signs of interrupted daily life only where appropriate to the script.
- Plausible present-day objects, architecture, clothing, and technology.
- No automatic castles, medieval villages, fantasy props, magic, alien technology, or invented monsters.
- Do not add zombies or other supernatural elements unless explicitly present in the user's source.

A post-apocalyptic setting is not the default story for every production. It is used when the supplied script requests it.

## Environment art direction
- Preserve believable real-world scale and practical navigation.
- Build modular buildings, road sections, interiors, street furniture, vehicles, vegetation, debris, and signage.
- Use layered depth: foreground, action plane, and background.
- Keep landmarks, doors, windows, furniture, and prop positions consistent between shots.
- Damage must have a cause or context in the script; do not randomly destroy every object.

## Cinematography
- Use composition, focal length, camera height, movement, lighting, atmosphere, and depth of field to support the scene.
- Keep important action readable.
- Prefer motivated camera movement and purposeful cuts.
- Avoid perpetual camera shake, excessive bloom, and visual clutter.
